# Workout Summary: "9 Exercises Is All You Need to Get Big, Strong & Athletic?"

**Source:** JT Performance (Jack Tyler), https://youtu.be/v306WBGi_Ec (9:32)
**Produced by:** `scripts/video-watcher.py`, from the caption track plus ~116 storyboard frames (one about every 5 s)

## The big idea

Athletes don't need specific exercises. They need specific **adaptations**. Training has **9 buckets**. Fill each one with whatever exercise suits you, because the bucket matters more than the exercise. Most people already fill buckets 1, 4, 7 and part of 8. Bucket 9 is what separates a gym athlete from a real athlete.

## The 9 buckets at a glance

| # | Bucket | Coach's pick | Weekly dose / target |
|---|--------|--------------|----------------------|
| 1 | Squat | Back, front or Zercher squat | Strength up to ~1.5× BW; diminishing returns past that |
| 2 | Sprint | Max-effort sprints | 120–300 m per session, 1–2×/week |
| 3 | Hinge | RDLs + back extensions | Load RDLs heavy, like a main lift |
| 4 | Pull | Pull-ups + chest-supported rows | 15+ bodyweight reps; advanced: 1.8× BW total load |
| 5 | Plyometrics | Hops (reactive) + broad jumps (max) | ~30 plyo reps + ~10 max jumps |
| 6 | Upper power | Smith-machine bench throws | Must be **thrown** and **plyometric** |
| 7 | Push | Bench press + push press | Bench ≥ 1.25× BW (men) |
| 8 | Conditioning | ESD intervals, off-feet | 2–3 sessions/week at elevated HR |
| 9 | Software (skill) | Play your sport | Be fresh for it; schedule around it |

## Bucket by bucket

### 1. Squat (0:50)
- Any variation works (back, front, Zercher). It's the best builder of big, strong, powerful legs. Squat strength correlates with sprint times, jump height and collision power.
- **Diminishing returns** (on-screen table):
  - **< 1.5× BW:** high transfer to speed and power. Keep building.
  - **1.5–2× BW:** moderate transfer. Returns taper.
  - **> 2× BW:** "be smart." Weigh the fatigue cost.
- Don't grind squats to failure. Fatigue blocks power development, and he moved worst right after his all-time squat PB.

### 2. Sprint (1:43)
- For about 90% of people, the time and energy beyond squat strength should go to sprinting.
- Sprints are the best plyometric there is: **3–5× BW forces in < 0.2 s** every step. When done properly, they're also the best protection against hamstring tears.
- Momentum (body weight × speed) predicts collision dominance better than power cleans do.
- **Quality beats quantity.** Don't treat sprints as cardio. His Olympic sprinter did 3 reps with 10–15 min rest between them.
- **Sprint session guide** (on-screen):
  - Distance per session: **120–300 m** (most athletes: ~150 m)
  - Distance per sprint: **10–60 m**
  - Sessions per week: **1–2**
  - Rest: **30–60 s per 10 m sprinted**
- Sprinting before you're ready carries injury risk, which is what bucket 3 is for.

### 3. Hinge (3:02)
- Favourite: **RDLs + back extensions**. They train the hamstrings at length better than deadlifts.
- Nordics are overrated: a 2026 paper found RDLs produce ~80% more biceps femoris force. Up to 84% of hamstring injuries hit the biceps femoris long head.
- **Treat RDLs as a main lift.** Most people load them too light to get this benefit.

### 4. Pull (3:43): do both
- **Vertical:** pull-ups, any grip. They also test relative strength.
  - Goal: **15 strict reps**. If he can't hit 15, he knows his diet is slipping.
  - Train them **weighted**. Advanced athletes should pull **> 1.8× BW** (body + added load).
- **Horizontal:** rows for the whole upper back. **Chest-supported rows** cause zero lower-back fatigue, so they're easier to program than barbell rows.

### 5. Plyometrics (4:25): not box jumps
- Real plyometrics need **fast ground contact (< 250 ms)** and **elastic energy storage**. Train your legs like springs.
- Hops are a great predictor of athleticism.
- **Jumps train power; plyos build springs.** Do both.
- Weekly dose: **~30 plyometric reps** (hops) + **~10 maximal jumps**. Favourite jump: the **broad jump**, which correlates with sprint speed.

### 6. Upper-body power (5:12)
A good upper-power exercise has two boxes to tick:
1. **Throw it** (the object or yourself). No deceleration at the top. That's why he dislikes landmine punches and speed bench. A 2018 study found bench throws beat speed bench for strength and power because they were performed about 2× faster.
2. **Plyometric** catch-and-release, which builds an upper-body spring.
- Pick: **Smith-machine bench throws**.
- No Smith machine? Use **partner drops** (catching a med-ball dropped from above) or **banded/plyo push-ups**.
- He rarely uses med-ball slams.

### 7. Push (6:22)
- **Bench** (barbell, dumbbell or incline) is the "upper-body armour" for contact sport.
- **Bench standards** (on-screen):

  | | Novice | Inter | Adv | Elite |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Men | 0.75× BW | 1.25× BW | 1.5× BW | 1.75× BW |
  | Women | 0.5× BW | 0.75× BW | 1× BW | 1.25× BW |

  Target: **at least 1.25× BW** (intermediate, men).
- **Push press**: full-body power from legs to arms (punches, hand-offs, tackles). Do it like a weightlifter: set up tall, dip with control, drive aggressively.
  - Good technique means you push press **20–30% more than your strict press**.

### 8. Conditioning (7:17): the ESD method
- "No point having the body of a Ferrari with the engine of a lawn mower."
- Elevate your heart rate for sustained periods **2–3×/week**. Prefer **off-feet** work, because your legs already take a beating from sprints, jumps, squats and sport.
- **ESD protocol** (from "Pete Bowridge" of Bristol Bears, spelled as the auto-captions have it), 1–2×/week:
  - **4 min work at 80–85% of max HR → 1 min rest**
  - **6–12 rounds**
  - Use an Assault/Airdyne bike, rower or SkiErg, ideally rotating between all three.
  - His athletes have set running PBs doing zero running, just this.

### 9. Software: skill and play (8:12), the most important bucket
- Capacity (strength, speed, power, conditioning) is the car. Skill is the driver. The biggest upgrade is usually learning to steer.
- Skill comes from thousands of reps and play, and **you must be fresh to build it**. Fatigue blocks skill development.
- **Rule:** no sprints, heavy legs or hard conditioning the **day before sport training**.
- On-screen: **leave ~48 h** between speed/power work, heavy strength work and sport.
- Build your week around your sport, not your bench day. If you don't play a sport, join one.

## Sample week (my construction from his rules)

The video points to a separate video for his own split. The week below is my example. It applies the rules above for someone who plays sport on Saturday.

| Day | Session | Contents |
|-----|---------|----------|
| Mon | **Speed + Lower** | Sprints 150 m total (e.g. 5×30 m, ~2–3 min rest) · Hops 3×10 · Broad jumps 5×2 · Squat 3–4×3–5 · RDL 3×6 |
| Tue | **Upper** | Smith bench throws 4×3 · Bench 4×5 · Weighted pull-ups 4×5 · Chest-supported row 3×10 · Push press 3×4 |
| Wed | **Conditioning (off-feet)** | ESD: 6–8 × (4 min @ 80–85% HRmax / 1 min rest), rotating bike / row / ski |
| Thu | **Speed + Lower (light) + Upper power** | Sprints 120 m (e.g. 6×20 m) · Hops 3×5 · Back extensions 3×12 · Partner drops or plyo push-ups 3×5 · Pull-ups (bodyweight) 2×max |
| Fri | **Fresh / Skill** | Off, or easy skill practice. No sprints, heavy legs or hard conditioning. |
| Sat | **Sport** | Play. |
| Sun | **Rest / Optional ESD** | Second ESD session if needed for 2–3 total HR exposures (with Sat sport). |

Weekly totals: sprint 270 m over 2 sessions · plyos ~45 hop contacts (cut to 30 if new to it) · 10 max jumps · conditioning 2–3 exposures · every bucket hit at least once.

## Quick self-test standards

- Squat: **1.5× BW** (beyond that, prioritise speed)
- Bench: **1.25× BW** (men) / **0.75× BW** (women)
- Pull-ups: **15 strict reps**; weighted total **1.8× BW** (advanced)
- Push press: **20–30% over strict press**
